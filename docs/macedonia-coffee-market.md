# Macedonia Coffee Market — Knowledge Note

> Background research for the HORECA / Café Station 1™ context in North Macedonia.
> Primary source: European Coffee Trip — "Barista Stories: Jerry Kaandorp of Jeronimo
> Specialty Coffee, Skopje / Aalsmeer" by Lukasz Galecki, 13 May 2026.
> (Barista Stories series sponsored by PUQ.)

## Jerry Kaandorp — profile

- **Role:** Self-taught **owner, roaster, and mentor** of **Jeronimo Specialty Coffee**.
- **Experience:** ~**12.5 years** in the coffee industry; a self-described lifelong coffee addict.
- **Dutch roots:** Grew up in the 1970s–80s with Douwe Egberts Roodmerk coffee and the
  Moccamaster in every Dutch kitchen — scooping grounds into the filter is his first coffee memory.
- **Philosophy / role model:** Follows his own path; reveres **Alfred Peet**, whom he calls the
  "Godfather of Specialty Coffee."
- **Personal:** Met his wife **Ivona** while working at **Radio Aalsmeer**; fell for her and the
  Balkans at once. Married **32 years**. Lifelong passion for **boats and open water** ("Dutch DNA").

## Jeronimo Specialty Coffee

- **Two homes:** A roastery + coffee bar with roots in **both Aalsmeer (Netherlands)** and
  **Skopje (North Macedonia)** — two very different markets under one brand.
- **Mission:** Source exceptional coffees, roast them with care, and make specialty coffee
  **approachable and affordable** for everyone.
- **Location choice — Aerodrom, not the centre:** Deliberately opened in **Aerodrom**, a relaxed
  residential Skopje neighbourhood, rather than the trendy city centre — so specialty coffee
  becomes part of **daily life**, not a one-off destination.
- **Origins:** Started by selling coffee roasted in the Netherlands, but Jerry wanted to roast
  **fresh in Macedonia**. The **pandemic delayed** the original opening; he used the time to
  refine the concept before finding the right location.
- **Experience offered:** Storytelling "from plantation to roastery to cup," fresh-roast aroma,
  warm welcome. Appears at the **Skopje Coffee Festival**.
- **Balkan rhythm:** Cafés in the Balkans don't close at sundown — **Jeronimo is open till 10 pm**,
  with a terrace Jerry likens to "little Italy" or a romantic Paris terrace.
- **Growth:** Now scaling up — **working on franchises across Macedonia**.

## Two markets, one philosophy (NL vs. North Macedonia)

Jerry's core lesson from operating across both countries: **coffee is universal, but coffee
culture is deeply local.**

| | Netherlands | North Macedonia |
|---|---|---|
| What customers value | Origin, processing methods, **sustainability**, flavour nuances | **Hospitality, tradition, social connection** |
| How coffee is consumed | Specialty/analytical lens | People **take time**; coffee is woven into daily life & relationships |
| Selling approach | Same product/story/marketing **does not transfer 1:1** between markets | |

Takeaways he stresses: **listen before assuming**, treat cultural differences as opportunities,
stay adaptable, and respect local preferences while holding core values of **quality,
transparency, and craftsmanship**. Ultimately, "coffee is about people" and the desire for
connection is the same in both places.

## Early reception & education curve in Skopje

- The local coffee culture was already **deeply rooted**; specialty coffee was new and met with
  curiosity but **scepticism** ("Why does this taste different? Why lighter roasted? Why more complex?").
- Early days were about **education and trust** — sharing origin/processing/roasting stories.
- Today conversations have shifted: customers now ask about **specific origins and varieties**.
  What began as curiosity has grown into a genuine **coffee community**. Several specialty shops
  are now opening; Jerry is proud to be a **pioneer**.

## Misconceptions Jerry pushes back on

- **"Coffee quality is only about the roast."** In reality quality **starts at origin** — variety,
  altitude, soil, climate, harvesting, processing, storage, roasting, and brewing all shape the cup.
  Roasting can highlight or mask, but **cannot create quality that isn't in the green coffee**.
- His one message for everyone: **great coffee begins long before the roaster or café** — it's one
  of the most complex agricultural products in the world, the sum of countless decisions by
  farmers, processors, exporters, roasters, and brewers.

## Broader North Macedonia coffee context

- **Ottoman heritage:** Turkish coffee introduced by merchants ~15th century. Coffee is a
  fundamental part of social interaction and daily life.
- **Tursko Kafe (traditional Macedonian/Turkish coffee):** Strong, unfiltered, very finely ground
  Arabica, water, optional sugar — culturally central, akin to Balkan/Middle Eastern versions.
- **Growing Skopje specialty scene** (last decade, accelerating): Jeronimo, plus Dabov Specialty
  Coffee Macedonia, EDGE Specialty Coffee, Lusso Specialty Coffee, The Dude Specialty Coffee.

## Relevance to HORECA / Café Station 1™

- The market blends **deeply rooted traditional café culture** with a **fast-growing specialty
  segment** — both depend on reliable in-café equipment and long daily operating hours (open till
  ~10 pm), which raises the stakes on uptime.
- **Franchise expansion** (Jeronimo's next step) is a strong fit for a self-service troubleshooting
  support tool: consistent equipment reliability across many sites matters when scaling a brand
  with a lean team.
- Localization should honour the strong **hospitality/tradition** framing — coffee service in
  Macedonia is a social ritual and a relationship, not a transaction.
